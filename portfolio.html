My name is Kengia Elifuraha Kengia. I am a Laboratory Specialist with a strong focus on Quality Assurance, Quality Control, and the documentation systems that keep laboratory operations consistent, accurate, and compliant.

My professional journey began with a Diploma in Veterinary Laboratory Science and Technology from the Livestock Training Institute (2013–2016), where I developed a rigorous scientific foundation. I then built practical competence through hands-on laboratory roles — from internship at the Tanzania Veterinary Laboratory Agency, to oil quality control at Mashati Oil Investment Limited, to my current senior role at Salibaba Pellets | Hill Group of Companies, where I have served as Senior Quality Assurance specialist since March 2019.

In my current role, I oversee all quality activities for an active animal feed production operation — managing laboratory personnel, reviewing and updating SOPs, analyzing NIRS-based proximate data, verifying raw materials and finished products against specifications, and applying root cause analysis when quality deviations occur.

What motivates me most is the understanding that laboratory quality is not just about passing tests — it is about building systems that protect product integrity consistently, day after day. A well-written SOP, a properly calibrated instrument, and a thorough investigation into a non-conformity are not bureaucratic tasks; they are the backbone of scientific credibility.

My Approach to Quality

Quality is not a single act — it is a system of interlocking disciplines. My approach to laboratory quality is built on six interconnected pillars, each supported by practical experience and documented procedures from my work at Salibaba Pellets | Hill Group and previous employers.

01

Quality Assurance

Preventing problems before they occur by building robust systems, clear procedures, and consistent practices across the laboratory.

In Practice at Hill Feed LabDeveloping and maintaining the Hill Feed Laboratory SOP Manual — ensuring all procedures are documented, current, and followed by all personnel.
02

Quality Control

Detecting and controlling quality deviations through systematic laboratory testing at every critical point — raw materials, in-process, and finished products.

In Practice at Hill Feed LabRunning NIRS proximate analysis on samples taken from at least two locations per batch every hour — generating real data to verify product meets nutritional specifications.
03

Documentation

Ensuring traceability, consistency, and reliable records so that every test result can be traced back to its origin, method, equipment, and personnel.

In Practice at Hill Feed LabDocumented sample labeling protocol requires Sample ID, date/time, source (batch number, manufacturer) — applied to every sample before analysis.
04

Root Cause Analysis

Investigating the true underlying cause of a quality problem — not treating symptoms but understanding why a deviation occurred in the first place.

In PracticeApplying troubleshooting and root cause identification as a documented responsibility in quality activities — ensuring corrective actions address the actual cause.
05

CAPA

Corrective and Preventive Actions — correcting existing problems AND putting systems in place to prevent their recurrence.

In Practice at Hill Feed LabUrease Activity SOP includes defined decision criteria — if soya is under-processed or over-processed, adjustment actions are triggered based on objective test results (red dot count).
06

Continuous Improvement

Improving laboratory processes, reliability, and efficiency through systematic review, data analysis, and feedback loops between quality and production.

In PracticeProviding analytical feedback and interpretation to the Production Manager — creating a quality-to-production communication loop that drives ongoing product improvement.

Case Study 02
Soya Bean Processing Quality Control Using Urease Activity (Qualitative Method)
01 — Challenge
The Problem

Soya bean is a critical protein ingredient in animal feed. Under-processed soya contains anti-nutritional factors (urease activity) that reduce feed efficiency and harm animal health. Over-processed soya also loses nutritional value.

02 — Objective
Goal

Determine objectively whether soya from the extruder or expeller is raw, under-processed, well-processed, or over-processed — and trigger action accordingly.

03 — My Role
Responsibility

Develop and implement a documented SOP (SOP 005) for urease activity determination. Train laboratory personnel. Interpret results and communicate findings to production.

04 — Approach
Method

Qualitative urease activity test using Cresol Red indicator and urea — observing colour change and red dot count at defined time intervals (90 sec and 5 min) against established criteria.

07 — Action
Decision Criteria

1–7 red dots = well cooked. 8–12 = acceptable but adjust machine. >12 dots = unacceptable — reprocess. Colour change before 90s = raw/under-cooked — reject.

📋

Lesson: A simple, low-cost qualitative test, when properly documented in an SOP with clear decision criteria, becomes a powerful quality control tool. The chemical check (raw soya on dish edge) built into the SOP ensures reagent integrity is verified before each test — this is Good Laboratory Practice in action.

The following are professional summaries of the documented procedures — no confidential SOP content is published verbatim.

SOP 001
Laboratory Safety Procedures
Ensures health and safety of all laboratory personnel and prevents accidents while handling animal feed samples, chemicals, and equipment. Covers PPE requirements, workplace safety, chemical handling, and waste disposal.
SafetyPPEChemical HandlingRisk Assessment
SOP 002
Sample Collection and Handling
Standardizes the process for collecting, labelling, storing, and handling animal feed samples. Includes representative sampling from ≥2 locations per batch, every hour. Requires Sample ID, date/time, and source labeling on every sample.
SamplingTraceabilityStorageTransportation
SOP 003
Grain Moisture Determination
Covers moisture content testing of grain materials using the Mini GAC Plus moisture meter. Includes calibration verification, device startup, grain type selection, sample loading, result reading, and post-test calibration check.
MoistureGrain TestingCalibrationMini GAC Plus
SOP 004
Proximate Analysis — NIRS Method
Rapid non-destructive proximate analysis using FOSS DS2500 NIRS analyzer with ISIScan Nova software. Covers daily/weekly maintenance, instrument startup, diagnostic runs, check sample protocol, analysis procedure, scan export via Mosaic, and upload to Evonik AMINONIR Advanced for amino acid profiling.
NIRSDS2500ProximateAmino AcidsISIScan Nova
SOP 005
Urease Activity — Qualitative Method
Assesses soya bean processing quality (extruder or expeller output) using Cresol Red indicator and urea. Objective decision criteria determine whether soya is raw, under-processed, well-processed, or over-processed. Built-in reagent integrity check included.
Soya QualityUreaseQualitativeCAPA Triggers
SOP 006
Pellet Durability Index (PDI)
Measures the mechanical durability of feed pellets using a tumbling machine. Pellets are tumbled for a set duration, sieved, and weighed. PDI is calculated as the percentage of intact pellets remaining after tumbling. Determines resistance to breakage during handling and transport.

Laboratory QA/QC
Why Every Laboratory Test Starts Before You Touch the Sample
Laboratory quality is often discussed in terms of instruments and methods. But in my experience, the most critical quality decisions happen before a sample even reaches the analyst — at the point of collection, labeling, and storage. A representative sample collected poorly produces accurate analysis of the wrong material.
Quality Takeaway: Sampling is a laboratory procedure. It must be controlled, documented, and validated like any other analytical method.
By Kengia Kengia | Laboratory Specialist | QA & QC

Soya Processing Quality: Why 1–7 Red Dots Matters More Than You Think
In animal feed production, soya bean is one of the most critical protein sources — and one of the most quality-sensitive ingredients. Under-processed soya carries anti-nutritional factors that reduce feed efficiency and harm animal performance. Over-processed soya loses lysine availability. The urease activity test gives us a simple, objective answer.
Quality Takeaway: A low-cost qualitative test with clear, documented decision criteria is more valuable than a complex test with no defined action threshold.

ISO & GLP
Root Cause Analysis in the Animal Feed Lab: Going Beyond the Obvious
When a batch of feed fails specification, the first instinct is often to retest. But retesting without investigation addresses nothing — if the root cause is a formulation error, a raw material deviation, or a calibration drift, it will happen again. True quality control means stopping and asking why before taking action.
Quality Takeaway: A corrective action that does not address the root cause is not a corrective action — it is a delay of the next failure.
By Kengia Kengia | Laboratory Specialist | QA & QC
